# S2: flux cut (does C mislabel equilibrium "rocks"?)

This file was written on 2026-10-22, **before** any S2 code or result existed. The pilot and results sections at the end were added later.

## Question (ROADMAP S2)
C (memory, I(A_t; A_{t+τ})) is the best predictor of viability in S1. But a "rock" also has high memory: a bistable
unit at equilibrium stays in its well for a long time and does nothing to stay alive. A dissipative structure stays
alive only because of a drive. S2 asks whether C rates rocks as high as drive-sustained structures, and whether a flux
cut (drive off) tells them apart.

## Why the ROADMAP model does not work, and what replaces it
- In the Stuart–Landau oscillator, the drive is the rotation term. Rotation does not change the amplitude dynamics, so a
  flux cut cannot change persistence of the amplitude.
- More generally, take an overdamped 2D system dz = (−∇U + F_nc) dt + noise, with F_nc a non-conservative drive
  (divergence free). The divergence of the drift is −ΔU. If U is convex, the Bendixson criterion forbids closed orbits.
  So a drive alone cannot create a cycle there. The cycle must come from U, and then it is a rock again.
- **Replacement:** an inertial (underdamped) oscillator with active Rayleigh friction, the "energy depot" model of active
  Brownian particles [SchweitzerEbelingTilch98, Romanczuk12]. At low speed the friction is negative, so the drive pumps
  energy in. With the drive off, the friction is the normal positive constant, and the particle is at equilibrium.

## Units (each unit is one structure with state z = (x, v))
dx = v dt, dv = (−U′(x) − γ(v) v) dt + √(2 γ₀ T) dW, with γ₀ = 1 and T = 0.1 for all units.

| Class | n | U(x) | γ(v) on | γ(v) off | Drawn parameters |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| rock | 60 | h (x² − 1)² | γ₀ | γ₀ (same) | h/T log-uniform in [1.5, 4] |
| oscillator | 60 | ω₀² x² / 2 | γ₀ − α + β v² | γ₀ | ω₀ log-uniform in [0.5, 2], α uniform in [1.5, 3], v₀ uniform in [0.7, 1.5], β = (α − γ₀)/v₀² |
| plain well (control) | 30 | k x² / 2 | γ₀ | γ₀ (same) | k log-uniform in [0.5, 2] |

The off dynamics of every unit satisfies the fluctuation–dissipation relation (constant friction γ₀, noise 2γ₀T),
so with the drive off every unit relaxes to the Boltzmann law ∝ exp(−(v²/2 + U)/T) [Kramers40].
Rocks and plain wells have no drive, so their on and off dynamics are the same.

## Measures
- **Viability set:** the highest-density region of the unit's own on-stationary law that holds 95 % of the mass
  (p_out = 0.05). For a Gaussian law this is the Mahalanobis ball of E3, so the definition generalises E3.
  The density is a 2D histogram (80 × 80 bins) from 4000 walkers after a burn-in of 200 time units.
- **V_on, V_off:** mean exit time from the set (exposure / exits), started from on-stationary points inside the set,
  then run with on or off dynamics. 2000 walkers, t_max = 500, Euler–Maruyama with dt = 0.01.
- **Flux ratio** R = V_on / V_off. For rocks and plain wells the same random numbers are used, so R = 1 exactly (a code check).
- **C** = I(z_t; z_{t+τ}), τ = 1, KSG estimator (k = 4) on 4000 independent pairs (one pair per walker) [Kraskov04].
- **TC** = I(x_t; v_t), KSG. **Φ_TC** = C − 0.25 TC (P = 0, because a unit has no environment here).
- **Drive power** (description only) = mean of (α − β v²) v² over the on-stationary law. It is 0 for rocks and plain wells.

## Rules (fixed before the run)
Bootstrap: 1000 resamples of units within each class. AUC = P(score of a random rock > score of a random oscillator).

| Rule | Question | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | Does the flux cut separate the classes? | "yes" if the median log R of oscillators > log 2 (CI low > log 2). Rocks and plain wells have R = 1 by construction. |
| F2 (main) | Does C rate rocks above oscillators at the same persistence? | Split all rocks and oscillators into 3 bins by tertiles of log V_on. In each bin with ≥ 5 units of each class, compute the AUC of C. F2 value = mean of these AUCs. "C mislabels rocks" if CI low > 0.5. "C favours oscillators" if CI high < 0.5. Else "no difference". "Not testable" if fewer than 2 bins qualify. |
| F3 | Same for Φ_TC | as F2, with Φ_TC in place of C |
| Gate (ROADMAP) | | If F2 and F3 both say "mislabels rocks", a dissipation term in Φ is mandatory. |
| F4 | Does C predict V_on across all units? | Spearman ρ(C, log V_on) over all 150 units. "yes" if CI low > 0. |

Also reported (description only): the unstratified AUC of C and of V_on, ρ(C, log R) among oscillators, and the drive power.

Expectations written before the run: F1 "yes". For F2 I expect "C mislabels rocks", because a rock in a deep well has
almost perfect memory of its well. I have no firm expectation for F3 and F4.

## Pilot (planned before the code)
Before the full run: 6 oscillators, 2 rocks. Check F1's condition (V_on / V_off > 2) and that the V_on ranges of
rocks and oscillators overlap. If the pilot fails, I change the design, write down the change here, and do not run F2–F4 on the pilot data.

## Design change before the pilot (2026-10-22, found by a unit test, before any S2 result)
The planned viability set (80 × 80 raw histogram from 4000 samples) fits the noise. It held 100 % of its own samples
but only 79 % of fresh samples. New set: 40 × 40 bins, Gaussian smoothing with σ = 1 bin, 16000 samples. Fresh samples
then fall inside in 95–97 % of cases for a rock, an oscillator and a plain well. Start points for exits and pairs for C
come from separate walkers (not the 16000 that build the set). Nothing else changes.

## Pilot 1 result and second design change (2026-10-22)
Pilot 1 (`results/e6_pilot.md` was overwritten by pilot 2, numbers kept here): 4 of 6 oscillators had V_on/V_off from
3.6 to 7.1, and both rocks had exactly 1.00. V_on was 6–10 for oscillators and 8–9 for rocks, so the ranges overlap.
But 2 oscillators had V_on/V_off = 0.01. A check showed why: their viability set contains the center (x, v) = (0, 0),
where the off dynamics settles. For both, v₀/√T ≈ 2.6, so the noise fills the hole in the cycle. Such a unit does not
need the drive to stay in its set, so it is not a drive-sustained structure.
**Change:** v₀ is now drawn uniform in [1.0, 1.5] (v₀/√T ≥ 3.2) instead of [0.7, 1.5]. In the first draw, 2 of 6
oscillators had no hole. The rules F1–F4 do not change. No pilot data enters F2–F4.
*Note (added 2026-11-04, docs/code_review.md item 12):* v₀ is the speed at which the active friction is zero, not
the amplitude of the cycle. The energy balance over a harmonic cycle gives a peak speed of
√(4(α − γ₀)/(3β)) = (2/√3)·v₀ ≈ 1.155·v₀. So the ratios above understate the distance of the cycle from the centre
by 15 %: about 3.0 instead of 2.6 for the two pilot units, and ≥ 3.7 instead of ≥ 3.2 for the new range. The code is
not affected (start points relax for 200 time units), and the change of the v₀ range is still justified.
Pilot 2 (`results/e6_pilot.md`, new v₀ range): all 6 oscillators have V_on/V_off from 2.7 to 7.3, both rocks 1.00.
V_on is 7–10 for oscillators and 8–9 for rocks. The pilot passes, so the full run starts with these settings.

### What S2 shows
1. **The flux cut works.** Cutting the drive shortens the life of every oscillator, by a factor of about 5.5 (median).
   Passive units do not change. So V_on/V_off tells drive-sustained structures from passive ones in this ensemble.
2. **C does not rate rocks above oscillators.** Every oscillator has a higher C than every rock. The gate is not
   triggered, so S2 gives no reason to add a dissipation term to Φ.
3. **But this rock class is a weak test of "memory without organisation".** Two post-hoc findings limit the result:
   - Most rock exits go through the outer edge of the set, not over the barrier. So V measures large fluctuations, not the loss of the well memory.
   - At τ = 1, C of a rock measures motion inside the well. A deeper well is stiffer and forgets faster inside it, so it has a lower C (ρ −0.98), although it lives longer (ρ +0.96).
   So C and V run opposite inside the rock class, and across all units C does not predict V (F4).
4. The high C of oscillators comes from their predictable phase: the state at t fixes the state at t + τ well. This is
   memory of a motion that the drive keeps up. In this ensemble C sees it, but C cannot tell drive from no drive by itself.
   The flux ratio can.

### Limits and next test
- The viability set (95 % highest-density region) puts most of the boundary at the outer edge for rocks.
- C is measured at τ = 1 only. Well memory shows at longer τ.
- A sharper rock test: define a rock as alive while it stays in its current well, and measure C at a τ near the well-switch time. This is not done.
- One drive model (Rayleigh friction) and one temperature.

## S2b: a sharper rock test (rules fixed 2026-10-23, before any S2b code or result)
**Why.** In S2, rocks left their set mostly through the outer edge, and C at τ = 1 measured motion inside the well.
So S2 did not test "memory without organisation". S2b changes the viability set and the lag τ so that both measure memory.

**Units.** The same 60 rocks and 60 oscillators as S2 (same seed). Plain wells are left out: they have no memory to lose.

**Viability (new):**
- Rock: alive while it stays in its current well. It starts in a well core (|x| > 0.5) and dies when it reaches the core of
  the other well (x on the other side with |x| > 0.5). So V is the mean well-switch time.
- Oscillator: alive while it stays on its cycle. E = v²/2 + ω₀²x²/2. E_cyc = median E of its on-stationary law. It is alive
  while E > 0.25 E_cyc, and dies when E falls below 0.25 E_cyc.
- Same estimator as before (exposure / exits), 2000 walkers from the on-stationary law, started only in the live region.
  t_max = 3000, dt = 0.01. For rocks the on and off dynamics are the same, so V_off = V_on by construction.

**Lag.** τ* = the median V of the 60 rocks under the new viability. It is computed from V only, before any C is computed.
C(τ*) = I(z_t; z_{t+τ*}) for every unit, KSG (k = 4), 4000 independent pairs. TC as in S2. Φ_TC = C(τ*) − 0.25 TC.

**Rules** (bootstrap 1000 resamples within each class; AUC = P(rock score > oscillator score)):

| Rule | Question | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| G0 (validity) | Does the rock V now measure well memory? | "valid" if ρ(h, log V) over rocks has CI low > 0. If not valid, G2–G4 are reported but not interpreted. |
| G1 | Does the flux cut still separate the classes? | "yes" if the median log(V_on/V_off) of oscillators has CI low > log 2. |
| G2 (main) | Does C(τ*) rate rocks above oscillators at the same V? | Same stratified AUC as F2 (tertiles of log V over rocks and oscillators, bins with ≥ 5 of each class, ≥ 2 bins). Labels: "C mislabels rocks", "C favours oscillators", "no difference", "not testable". |
| G3 | Same for Φ_TC | as G2 |
| Gate (ROADMAP) | | If G2 and G3 both say "mislabels rocks", a dissipation term in Φ is mandatory. |
| G4 | Does C(τ*) track rock memory? | ρ(C(τ*), log V) over rocks. "yes" if CI low > 0. |

Also reported (description only): the unstratified AUC of C(τ*) and of log V, and C with a per-unit lag τ = V/2.

**Expectation written before the run.** At a long lag, an oscillator loses its phase by phase diffusion, but a rock keeps
its well. So I expect "C mislabels rocks" in G2, and G4 "yes".

**Pilot.** 4 rocks (the two lowest and two highest h) and 4 oscillators: V only, no C. Check that the log V ranges of rocks
and oscillators overlap. If they do not overlap, I change the oscillator threshold (0.25 E_cyc) before any C is computed,
and I record the change here.

### S2b pilot (2026-10-23, V only, `results/e6b_pilot.md`)
Rocks (lowest and highest h): V = 51, 53, 256, 294. Oscillators: V_on = 20, 52, 104, 112, with V_off = 1.8–2.3.
The log V ranges overlap (about 50–112), so no change is needed. The full run uses the settings above.

### S2b results (added 2026-10-23, after the run)
Report: `results/e6b_report.md`. Data: `results/e6b_dataset.pkl`. 120 units. τ* = 118.3 (median rock V). Every unit had
at least 1642 exits.

| Rule | Value [95% CI] | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| G0: ρ(h, log V), rocks | +1.00 [+0.99, +1.00] | valid |
| G1: median log(V_on/V_off), oscillators | +3.14 [+2.79, +3.41] (about 23 times) | yes |
| G2: stratified AUC of C(τ*) | 0.76 [0.60, 0.86], 2 bins | C mislabels rocks |
| G3: stratified AUC of Φ_TC | 1.00 [1.00, 1.00] | Φ_TC mislabels rocks |
| Gate | | **triggered** |
| G4: ρ(C(τ*), log V), rocks | +0.76 [+0.60, +0.88] | yes |

Description only: unstratified AUC of C(τ*) 0.71. Median C(τ*): rocks 0.012 nats, oscillators −0.0004 nats.
With each unit's own lag (τ = V/2) the AUC is **0.18**: C then favours oscillators (median 0.34 against 0.06).

### Post-hoc checks (done after the results were seen)
| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Noise floor of C(τ*) (sd over oscillators, true value about 0) | 0.0093 nats. Only 38 % of rocks lie above 2 sd. |
| Rock C(τ*) against a two-state well-memory model (switch rate 1/V, I of a binary symmetric channel) | correlation 0.92, medians 0.009 (model) and 0.012 (measured) |
| What drives G3 | TC: oscillators 0.29, rocks about 0. The −0.25 TC term alone puts every oscillator below every rock. |

### What S2b shows
1. **The test is now valid.** Rock life is the well-switch time and follows the barrier height (ρ = +1.00).
2. **The flux cut separates the classes clearly.** Cutting the drive shortens oscillator life about 23 times. Rocks do not change.
3. **At a common long lag, C rates rocks above oscillators.** A rock keeps a small memory of its well (about 0.01 nats,
   as the two-state model predicts). An oscillator loses its phase. So the gate rule triggers: by ROADMAP decision point 3,
   Φ needs a dissipation term.
4. **The trigger is weak, and three limits apply:**
   - The rock signal is near the estimator noise (0.012 against a noise sd of 0.009).
   - G3 comes from the TC term, not from C.
   - The verdict depends on the lag. With each unit's own lag, C favours oscillators (AUC 0.18).
5. **What is robust:** C alone cannot tell drive-sustained persistence from passive memory. Its verdict changes with τ.
   The flux ratio V_on/V_off separates the two classes in S2 and in S2b with no overlap.

### Consequence for Φ
A dissipation-sensitive term is needed. The flux ratio needs a second simulation, so a direct candidate is the
entropy production (or drive power) of the structure. That term is 0 for rocks and positive for drive-sustained units.
Testing such a term is the next step. It is not done.
