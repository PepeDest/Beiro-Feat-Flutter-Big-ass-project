# S3: information that is used (agency and a hidden-state environment)

This file was written on 2026-10-22, **before** any S3 code or result existed. The results section at the end was added after the run.

## Question (ROADMAP S3)
In S0–S1 the structures were passive: nothing used their information about the environment, and P had a small effect.
The explanation was "information pays only when it is used". S3 tests that explanation. It builds a small organism that
senses a hidden environmental state and acts against it. Then it breaks the link between sensing and acting, and it
measures how much viability falls.

## Model: a linear homeostat
All parts are linear OU processes (dx = −B x dt + √(2D) dW). So every information term stays exact (gaussian.py).

| Variable | Equation | Role |
|---|---|---|
| h | dh = −h/T_h dt + noise, var(h) = 1 | hidden environmental state (the disturbance). The organism cannot change it. |
| s | ds = k_s (h − s) dt + noise, noise variance about σ_s² | sensor |
| m | dm = k_m (s − m) dt + small noise | internal state (memory) |
| u | du = k_u (−L m − u) dt + small noise | actuator. L is the loop gain. |
| v | dv = (−k_v v + h + u) dt + noise | viability variable. h pushes it, u can push back. |

The organism is A = {s, m, u, v}. The environment is h.
L = 1 cancels h in the ideal case (fast parts, no sensor noise). L = 0 means no feedback.

**Deviation from the ROADMAP text.** The ROADMAP suggests a two-state switching regime. S3 uses a slow Gaussian hidden
state instead. This keeps all information terms exact. A switching regime is left for a later step.

**Organism draws** (one world per organism, 400 organisms, seed 2026):
T_h log-uniform in [2, 20], σ_s log-uniform in [0.1, 3], k_s, k_m and k_u log-uniform in [0.5, 5], k_v log-uniform in
[0.2, 1], L uniform in [0, 1.5]. Noise of m and u: D = 0.01. Noise of v: D = 0.05 k_v.

**Three versions of each organism:**
1. *intact*: as above.
2. *scrambled*: the actuator reads m′ instead of m. m′ comes from a copy of the chain (h′ → s′ → m′) with the same
   parameters and independent noise. So the actuator gets an input with the same statistics, but with no information
   about h. This is the scramble intervention of Kolchinsky and Wolpert (2018).
3. *no feedback*: L = 0.

**Viability.** The organism is alive while |v| < c. c is fixed for each organism: c = 1.2816 × sd(v) of its
no-feedback version. So the no-feedback version spends 20 % of its time outside. V = mean first-passage time out of the
set, from the stationary law conditioned on |v| < c. Same rate estimator as E3 (exposure / exits).
This is **not** the occupancy-matched Mahalanobis ball of E3. With an occupancy-matched set, a regulator that makes v
smaller also makes its own set smaller, and the benefit of regulation cancels. A fixed physical limit on v is the
normal meaning of homeostasis.
Simulation: exact propagator, dt = 0.05, 600 trajectories, t_max = 1000.

**Information features** (exact, nats, τ = 1, measured in the intact and in the scrambled version):
- P = I(A_t ; h_{t+τ}): predictive information about the hidden state.
- C = I(A_t ; A_{t+τ}).
- TC(A): multi-information of A (S1b).
- I(v; h) = I(v_t ; h_t): how much the disturbance shows in v.
- leak (description only, because the environment of A differs between versions).

## Rules (fixed before the run)
Organisms with fewer than 5 exits in any version are dropped. Their number is reported. Bootstrap: 1000 resamples of organisms.
VoI (value of information) = log V_intact − log V_scrambled, for each organism.

| Rule | Question | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | Is the information used? | "used" if mean VoI > 0 and its CI low > 0. Else "not used". |
| R2 | Does P's effect on V depend on the loop? (ROADMAP "done" criterion) | Regress log V on standardised (P, C, TC), once on intact and once on scrambled organisms. Use one scale (the SD over both sets). "depends on the loop" if β_P(intact) CI low > 0 and the paired difference β_P(intact) − β_P(scrambled) has CI low > 0. Else "does not depend". |
| Gate | ROADMAP key decision | If R1 is "used" and β_P(intact) has CI low ≤ 0, then the predictive term is not what drives viability. Then Φ must be redesigned around closure or autonomy. |
| R3 | Does P show usable information? | Spearman ρ(P_intact, VoI). "yes" if CI low > 0. |
| R4 | Good-regulator pattern | Spearman ρ(I(v; h)_intact, log V_intact). "present" if CI high < 0 (good regulation hides h from v). |
| R5 | Does Φ_TC rank organisms? | Spearman ρ(P + C − 0.25 TC, log V) on intact organisms. "supported" if CI low > 0. The scrambled value is reported too. |

Description only (no rule): VoI against the no-feedback version, the fraction of organisms with VoI > 0, leak, and
the drop count.

Expectations written before the run: R1 "used" for most organisms with L near 1 and low sensor noise. Organisms with
L well above 1 overcorrect, so VoI can be negative for them. R4 "present". I have no firm expectation for R2.

### Post-hoc checks (done after the results were seen)
**Not recomputed in phase C (2026-11-04).** These checks keep the old data (old exit check, 394 organisms).

1. **Time scale of h.** log T_h correlates with log V_intact (ρ = +0.44). I added log T_h to the R2 regression.
   Then β_P is +1.20 (intact) and +0.48 (scrambled). The partial ρ(P_intact, VoI | T_h) is +0.41. So T_h does not explain R2 or R3.
2. **R4 stays unexplained.** The partial ρ of I(v; h) with log V_intact is +0.26 given T_h. It is +0.29 given the
   no-feedback I(v; h), which sets the limit c. Neither check explains the positive sign.
   One idea is not tested: v follows h with a delay, so I(v_t; h_t) at equal times can miss the real pattern.
3. **P in the scrambled version still has a positive effect** (+0.38). So unused information still predicts a
   longer life in part. A possible reason: P also counts what v itself carries about h. I did not test this.

### What S3 shows
1. **Information pays when it is used.** Breaking the sensor → actuator link lowers log V by 1.27 on average (V falls about ~~3.6~~ 3.5 times; phase C, `_v2`).
2. **The effect of P depends on the loop.** This is the ROADMAP "done" criterion. β_P with a working loop is about
   twice β_P with a scrambled loop. The CI of the difference excludes 0.
3. **P shows usable information.** Organisms with more predictive information lose more when the loop is cut (ρ = +0.41).
4. **The gate is not triggered.** So the predictive term of Φ stays. ROADMAP decision point 2 says: keep P. Do not redesign Φ around closure only.
5. **The good-regulator pattern does not show in I(v_t; h_t)** in this ensemble. This needs a lagged measure before any claim.
6. **Φ_TC ranks organisms.** It does less well in the intact version (~~+0.20~~ +0.21, phase C `_v2`) than in the scrambled version (+0.39).
   With a working loop, the quality of the loop (L, sensor noise) matters. Φ_TC does not measure that quality.

### Limits
- One linear model, chosen by us. The hidden state is Gaussian, not a two-state switch.
- The viability limit is fixed per organism from its no-feedback version. Other choices can change the size of VoI.
- ~~6~~ 5 organisms (phase C, `_v2`) are dropped (censored). They are the best regulators.
- P, C and TC are measured at τ = 1 only.

## S3b: a lagged good-regulator measure for R4 (rules fixed 2026-10-22, before the analysis)
**Why.** R4 used I(v_t; h_t) at equal times and found the opposite sign. v follows h with a delay, so the equal-time
value can miss the dependence. Also, I now suspect a time-scale effect: when v carries much about the slow h, v itself
moves slowly, and a slow v leaves its limit less often at the same variance. This suspicion came after the S3 results.

**Data.** The 394 kept S3 organisms. Each organism is rebuilt from its stored parameters. V comes from the stored run.
No new simulation. Check before use: the rebuilt I(v_t; h_t) must equal the stored value (to 1e-9).

**Measures** (exact, from the lagged covariance C(δ) = e^{−Bδ} S):
- I_lag = max over δ in {0, 0.25, …, 20} of I(v_{t+δ}; h_t). Computed for the intact and the no-feedback versions.
- τ_v = integrated autocorrelation time of v = (B⁻¹ S)_vv / S_vv (exact for OU). If τ_v ≤ 0 for any organism, use the
  integral of |corr_v| over δ in [0, 50] instead, and report it.
- margin = c / sd(v) in the intact version (how many standard deviations v has before the limit).

**Rules** (Spearman, 1000 bootstrap resamples of organisms):

| Rule | Question | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| R4a | Does the lagged measure show the good-regulator pattern? | "present" if ρ(I_lag intact, log V_intact) has CI high < 0. Else "not present". |
| R4b | Do organisms that hide more of h from v live longer? | ΔI = I_lag(no feedback) − I_lag(intact). "present" if ρ(ΔI, log V_intact) has CI low > 0. |
| R4c | Does the time scale of v explain the positive R4 sign? | Partial ρ(I(v_t; h_t) intact, log V_intact), given log margin and log τ_v (rank residuals). "explained" if its CI includes 0 or lies below 0. Else "not explained". |

### What S3b shows
1. **The good-regulator pattern does not appear** in the lagged form either. Organisms that hide more of h from v do
   not live longer across this ensemble (R4b is slightly negative).
2. **The positive R4 sign comes mainly from the time scale of v.** At a fixed margin, a slow v leaves its limit less
   often (+0.71). v is slow when a slow h drives it, and then v also carries more information about h (+0.52).
3. **R4c says "explained", but the control is strong.** Margin and τ_v carry almost all of log V, so every information
   measure acts through them. R4c shows that I(v; h) has no effect of its own beyond them. It does not show that I(v; h)
   is special.
4. The good-regulator theorem (Conant and Ashby) says what a working regulator must contain. It does not say that a low
   I(v; h) must predict a longer life across different organisms. S3b is consistent with that reading.
