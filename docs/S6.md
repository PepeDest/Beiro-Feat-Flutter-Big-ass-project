# S6: heredity and replication (first test bed)

This file was written on 2026-11-03, **before** any S6 code or result existed. The pilot and results sections at the end were added later.

## Question (ROADMAP S6)
The theory (T3) needs a transmission term: a measured heredity fidelity in the Price equation. S1–S5 had no real division.
S6 starts with a physical system that divides on its own: Gray–Scott self-replicating spots [Pearson93].
This first test bed asks:
1. **Growth law.** Does the number of spots grow exponentially or polynomially? Exponential growth is a mark of
   replicators, and parabolic growth is a mark of limited ones [Eigen71, SzathmaryMaynardSmith95]. On a surface, only
   spots with free space next to them can divide, so I expect a polynomial (area-like) law.
2. **Native heredity.** Does a daughter spot resemble its parent in any trait of its own (here: its size at division)?
   I expect no: all spots relax to the same shape, so Gray–Scott replication has no heredity of its own.
3. **Positive control.** A neutral dye that a spot keeps and splits at division. If the pipeline cannot find heredity in
   the dye, a "no" for question 2 means nothing.
4. **Price terms with a measured fidelity** (the ROADMAP "done" criterion), using the dye as the trait.

## Model
- Gray–Scott on a 200 × 200 periodic grid, explicit Euler, dt = 1, grid spacing 1:
  u_t = D_u ∇²u − u v² + F (1 − u),  v_t = D_v ∇²v + u v² − (F + k) v,  D_u = 0.16, D_v = 0.08.
  Small noise on v each step (sd 5·10⁻⁴) breaks symmetry.
- Parameters: F = 0.0367, k = 0.0649 (a spot-splitting region). Fallback list if the pilot fails, in order:
  (0.035, 0.065), (0.030, 0.062).
- **Dye c** (neutral: it does not act back on u or v): c_t = D_c ∇²c − δ (1 − s) c, with D_c = 0.01, δ = 0.05,
  s = 1 inside spots (v > threshold) and 0 outside. So the dye stays in a spot, decays outside, and is split between
  the two daughters at division. A daughter that grows into empty space dilutes its dye. Trait: z = log(mean dye in the spot).
- Start: 4 spots at fixed, well-separated places. Each gets its own dye level, log-uniform in [0.5, 2].
- Run until the spot count has not grown for 2000 steps, at most 100 000 steps. 8 replicate runs (seeds 2026 + i).

## Spot tracking
Every 10 steps: spots = connected regions with v > 0.1 (with periodic wrap). A spot in the new frame continues a spot of
the old frame if they overlap. One old spot that overlaps two new spots is a **division** (parent → two daughters).
For each division, record the parent's area and dye trait at the last frame before it splits.
A **parent–daughter pair** is (parent's record at its division, daughter's record at the daughter's own division).

Expectations written before the run: R1 "polynomial", R2 "none found", R3 "pipeline detects heredity", R4 S ≈ 0 and T < 0
(dilution lowers z in each generation).

### Expectations that were wrong
- R1: I expected "polynomial". The exponential fit wins in every run, but only by 0.000 to 0.012 in R². The window
  (N from 8 to about 70) is less than one decade, so the two laws cannot be told apart there. The power exponent 1.87 is
  close to 2, the area law. **R1 does not discriminate.**
- R2: I expected "none found". The rule says "native heredity" (+0.26).
- R4: I expected S ≈ 0 for the neutral dye. S is +4.72, and T ≈ −S.

### Post-hoc checks (done after the results were seen)
| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| R2 with the parent's division time held fixed | partial ρ +0.18. Within 10 time bins: mean ρ +0.17 (range −0.01 to +0.33). |
| S within each generation (49 generation groups, parents that divide ≥ 3000 steps before the end) | weighted mean +0.47, against +4.72 pooled |

### What S6 (first test bed) shows
1. **Gray–Scott spots replicate:** from 4 seed spots to about 230 in each run.
2. **The pipeline measures heredity:** the dye passes from parent to daughter with ρ = 0.975. So a measured fidelity now
   enters the Price transmission term (the ROADMAP "done" criterion), with T = −5.25.
3. **But the Price terms are not clean in this population.** For a neutral trait, the selection term must be 0. It is
   +4.72. About 90 % of it comes from mixing generations: early parents carry more dye (dilution acts per generation)
   and also have more daughters that divide (the colony fills later). The rest (+0.47 within generations) is not explained.
   So in a growing, spatial population with overlapping generations, S and T must be computed per generation, and a
   neutral trait must serve as a negative control.
4. **Native heredity of spot size is weak and partly a time effect** (+0.26 raw, +0.17 at fixed time). A shared
   neighbourhood (crowding) can also make parent and daughter alike. That is not tested, so this is not evidence of
   heredity in the sense of T3.
5. **The growth-law test cannot decide** in this window (see R1).

### Limits
- One reaction-diffusion model and one parameter set. No active droplets (the second ROADMAP test bed).
- The dye heredity is put in by design. It is a positive control, not a property of Gray–Scott spots.
- The tracker counts overlap every 10 steps. One merge in 8 runs, so tracking errors are rare but not zero.
- No trait in this test bed changes the division rate, so real selection is not tested.

### Next test (not done)
Give a heritable trait an effect on division (for example, the dye lowers the local kill rate k), compute S and T per
generation, and use the neutral dye as the negative control in the same runs.

## S6b: a heritable trait that changes division (rules fixed 2026-11-04, before any S6b code or result)
**Why.** In S6 the dye was neutral and diluted each generation, and the pooled Price terms mixed generations. S6b
gives the spots a heritable trait with an effect on division, computes the Price terms per generation, and runs a
neutral control in the same design.

**Model.** The S6 Gray–Scott spots (F = 0.0367, k = 0.0649), now with two dyes a and b that compete inside spots:
a_t = D_c ∇²a + γ s a (1 − a − 2b) − δ (1 − s) a, and the same for b with a and b swapped. γ = 0.05, D_c = 0.01,
δ = 0.05, s = 1 inside spots. The strong cross-competition (factor 2) makes a spot settle on one dye: its **type** is
A if mean a > mean b in the spot, else B. Type A changes the local kill rate: k_eff = k − ε a.
- Start: 4 seed spots, 2 of type A and 2 of type B, on the two diagonals of the grid (which diagonal is A alternates between runs).
- Grid 300 × 300 (larger than S6, to get a longer growth window). Run until the spot count has not grown for 2000 steps, at most 150 000 steps.
- Numerics: the same equations, compiled with numba (float64). A test checks it against the S6 numpy code.

**Pilot (sets ε, before any main run).** Colonies of one type only (all A or all B), 4 runs each, for ε in
{0.001, 0.002, 0.003}. ε* = the smallest value that changes the median cycle time (birth to division) of A against B
by at least 10 %, while A colonies still reach ≥ 32 spots in 20 000 steps. The sign of the change gives the predicted
sign of selection: if A divides faster, selection on z = 1[type A] is predicted positive. If no ε passes, S6b stops and I report this.

**Main runs.** 32 runs at ε = ε* (selection) and 32 runs at ε = 0 (neutral control), on all 4 cores.

**Measures.** Generation g = number of divisions back to a seed spot. For each run and each generation, the parents of
generation g that divide at least 3000 steps before the end: w = number of their daughters that divide, z = 1[type A]
at division. S_g = Cov(w, z)/w̄, T_g = E[w (z̄_daughters − z)]/w̄. Pooled over generations (weights = number of parents),
then mean over runs, with a bootstrap CI over runs. Fidelity = P(daughter type = parent type).

| Rule | Question | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | Is the type inherited? | "high fidelity" if fidelity ≥ 0.95 in both conditions |
| H2 | Negative control: is S ≈ 0 at ε = 0? | "control clean" if the CI of S contains 0. If not clean, H3 is reported but not interpreted. |
| H3 | Selection at ε = ε*: does S have the predicted sign? | "selection detected" if the CI of S excludes 0 with the sign from the pilot. "wrong sign" if it excludes 0 with the other sign. Else "not detected". |
| H4 | Does the type frequency change in the predicted direction? | Final fraction of type A over the spots at the end. "yes" if its CI over runs excludes 0.5 on the predicted side (control: CI must contain 0.5) |
| H5 | Bookkeeping check of the Price identity | Per generation, w̄ Δz̄ = S + T with Δz̄ from the actual daughters. "exact" if the largest error < 1e-9 |
| H6 | Growth law, with the longer window of the 300 × 300 grid | Same rule as R1 (exponential against power fit), selection runs |

Expectations written before the run: H1 "high fidelity", H2 "control clean", H3 "selection detected", H4 "yes",
H5 "exact". H6: no firm expectation.

**Change of numerics before any S6b data (2026-11-04).** The rules above say "compiled with numba". numba was only 1.5
times faster than numpy on the 300 × 300 grid (14 ms per step). So the grid update now runs on the GPU (AMD RX 580,
OpenCL, float64, `src/vlife/gs_opencl.py`), 0.21 ms per step, and the CPU cores do the spot tracking. The equations do not
change. A test checks the GPU kernel against the numba kernel with the noise off (agreement to 1e-10), and the numba kernel
against the S6 numpy code. The GPU noise comes from a counter-based hash with Box–Muller (sd checked: 5.1e-4).

**Note on H5 (added 2026-11-04, docs/code_review.md item 4).** H5 cannot fail. The code computes Δz̄ as
Σ w·z̄_daughters / Σ w − z̄, with the same w and z̄_daughters as S and T, and S + T equals this expression for any numbers.
So the 1.1e-16 is rounding error only. H5 checks the arithmetic, not the bookkeeping (generations, daughters, types).
The verdict "exact" stays as recorded, but it must be read as an arithmetic check.
| H6: growth law | power fit wins in 32 of 32 runs, exponent median 1.92, window N 8 → 174 | | polynomial |

### What S6b shows
1. **Heredity is perfect, and selection works as predicted.** Type A divides 24 % faster, and the Price selection term is
   positive (+0.039 per generation). By the end, 72 % of the spots are type A, against 50 % in the control.
2. **The per-generation bookkeeping fixes the S6 problem.** With the neutral control, S is 0 within its CI (S6: +4.72
   pooled). ~~The Price identity holds to 1e-16 in every generation, so the bookkeeping is correct.~~
   **Superseded (2026-11-04):** the Price identity holds to 1e-16 in every generation, but this is true by construction
   (see the note on H5). It does not show that the bookkeeping is correct. The clean neutral control (H2) is the evidence
   for the bookkeeping.
3. **The transmission term is exactly 0**, because no spot ever changed type in 34 537 pairs. So this test bed measures
   selection with perfect heredity. It cannot test an error threshold [Eigen71]: that needs mutation.
4. **Growth is polynomial** (exponent 1.92, close to the area law 2). The longer window of the larger grid decides the
   question that S6 (R1) could not. Only spots at the edge of the colony have space to divide.

### Limits
- The heritable types are put in by design (two competing dyes). They are not a property of Gray–Scott spots.
- No mutation, so no test of fidelity against an error threshold.
- The trait acts through the kill rate only. The link to Φ (organisation) is not tested here.

## S6c: mutation and the error threshold (rules fixed 2026-11-04, before any S6c code or run)
**Why.** S6b had perfect heredity, so the transmission term was 0 and no error threshold could be tested [Eigen71].

**Mutation.** One way only: when a type-A spot divides, each daughter becomes type B with probability μ (its two dyes
are swapped inside the daughter, at the frame where the division is found). B never turns back into A. This is the
single-peak model of the error threshold.

**Prediction, from S6b data only (computed before any S6c run).**
- Per generation, an A parent does not have more dividing daughters than a B parent (ratio 0.997). The advantage of A is
  speed: in the S6b mixed colonies the median cycle is 1120 steps for A and 1350 for B, so T_A/T_B = 0.825.
- An A lineage keeps up with B only if ln(2(1 − μ))/T_A > ln 2/T_B. That gives **μ_c = 1 − 2^(T_A/T_B − 1) = 0.114
  [0.110, 0.118]** for regular cycle times. If cycle times were exponential, the threshold would be (1 − T_A/T_B)/2 = 0.088.
  Both are mean-field values. They ignore space: in these colonies only spots at the edge divide (S6b: polynomial growth).

**Runs.** All four seed spots are type A. ε = 0.001 (as in S6b), 300 × 300 grid, same stop rule. μ = μ_c × {0, 0.25, 0.5,
0.75, 1, 1.25, 1.5, 2, 3} = {0, 0.029, 0.057, 0.086, 0.114, 0.143, 0.171, 0.228, 0.342}. 20 runs per μ (180 runs), GPU
and 5 CPU workers. Price terms per generation as in S6b.

| Rule | Question | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | Is the mutation rate as set? Fraction of A parents' daughters that are B, per μ | "as set" if every μ lies in its 95 % binomial CI |
| M2 | Does the transmission term match the mutation? Predicted per generation: T = −μ E[w z]/w̄ | "matches" if the mean measured T is within 20 % of the mean predicted T for every μ ≥ 0.25 μ_c |
| M3 | Is there an error threshold? f(μ) = final fraction of type A | "threshold present" if f has CI low > 0.5 for μ ≤ 0.5 μ_c, and CI high < 0.1 for μ ≥ 2 μ_c |
| M4 | Where is it? μ̂ = the μ where f crosses 0.1 (linear interpolation of the run means) | "consistent with mean field" if μ̂ lies in [0.5, 2] × 0.114. Also reported: which mean-field value (0.114 or 0.088) is closer |

Expectations written before the run: M1 "as set", M2 "matches", M3 "threshold present". For M4 I expect μ̂ above 0.114,
because a finite run ends before a slowly declining A type is gone.

**Note before the S6c runs (2026-11-04).** A smoke run (μ = 0.2, 6000 steps) showed that counting only daughters that
divide again gives a low mutation fraction (0.11), because the slower B daughters often do not divide before the end.
M1 therefore counts **all** daughters of type-A parents (type at their own division, else at their last frame), as the
rule text says. In the smoke run this gives 32 of 187 (0.171), exactly the number of mutations drawn. Nothing else changes.

### What S6c shows
1. **A nonzero transmission term from real copying errors is now measured.** The mutation rate is as set, and the
   selection term grows with μ (more variation to select on).
2. **The transmission term matches the mutation rate when it counts all daughters** (within about 3 % at every μ). The
   pre-registered version counts only daughters that divide again. Mutant daughters divide less often before the end, so
   that version is smaller (50–60 %). The gap is selection against mutants at the offspring stage. My prediction formula
   ignored this, so M2 failed for a reason in the bookkeeping, not in the mutation.
3. **No sharp error threshold appears in these runs.** The fraction of A falls smoothly, and it crosses 0.1 only at
   μ = 0.33, about 3 times the mean-field μ_c.
4. **Finite run length alone does not explain this.** A mean-field model run for the same time keeps much less A than the
   colonies do (0.27 against 0.58 at μ_c). So the spatial colony protects the faster type. A likely reason: growth happens
   only at the colony edge, and the faster type stays at the edge. This was not tested.

### Limits
- About 16–22 generations per run, because the grid fills. A long-term (equilibrium) threshold needs longer runs or a
  larger grid.
- One-way mutation and one fitness effect (ε = 0.001).
- The mean-field comparison is post hoc and simple (fixed cycles, no space).
