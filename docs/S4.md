# S4: viability as population growth rate

This file was written on 2026-11-03, **before** any S4 code or result existed. The results section at the end was added after the analysis.

## Question (ROADMAP S4)
Does the population growth rate r rank structures like the mean exit time V? And can the Price equation measure a
transmission term once an explicit parent → offspring map exists?

## The birth rule, and why it is chosen this way
- If every structure gives birth at the same constant rate b and its life is exponential with mean V, then r = b − 1/V.
  Then r ranks structures exactly like V, by construction. A birth rule that depends on the structure would put the
  answer into the rule. So S4 uses one birth rule for all structures, and r differs from V **only through the shape of
  the survival curve l(a)**.
- **Rule:** an individual gives birth at constant rate b = 1 from its maturation age a_m on (age-structured model).
  The Euler–Lotka equation then gives r from 1 = b ∫_{a_m}^∞ e^{−ra} l(a) da [Lotka1907, Charlesworth94].
  The net reproductive number is R0 = b ∫_{a_m}^∞ l(a) da (offspring per lifetime).
- Two maturation settings: **a_m = 0** (r depends on the whole survival curve, close to V for exponential lives) and
  **a_m = median V of the world** (main setting: survival up to maturity counts, as in real life histories).
- **Deviation from the ROADMAP:** the ROADMAP says "birth rate from resources". These worlds have no resource, so the
  birth rate is the same for all structures. A resource model is left open.

## Survival curves
From the stored E3 exit data (600 trajectories per structure, censored at t_max): l(a) = fraction of valid trajectories
still inside at age a, on the dt grid. All censoring happens at t_max, so this equals the Kaplan–Meier estimate. After
t_max the curve continues with hazard 1/V. Structures with fewer than 5 exits are dropped, as in E3.

## Offspring map for the Price equation
Trait z = Φ_TC (S1b). Fitness w = R0 (main setting). Child of structure A: with probability 0.9 an exact copy, with
probability 0.1 a copy with one variable swapped for one outside variable (same size, every swap equally likely).
Then, per world [Price70, Frank95]:
- selection term S = Cov(w, z) / w̄,
- transmission term T = E[w · E(Δz)] / w̄, with E(Δz) = 0.1 × (mean z of all swaps of A − z(A)).

## Data
S1 baseline (90 worlds) for G1, G3 and G4. S1 baseline and p_out 0.2 (same worlds, wider limit) for G2.
Mean within-world Spearman ρ, cluster bootstrap over worlds (400 resamples).

### Post-hoc check (done after the results were seen)
Coefficient of variation of the exit times, over 5580 nearly uncensored structures: median ~~1.015~~ 1.037, 5–95 % range
~~0.95 to 1.09~~ 0.97 to 1.11 (phase C, recomputed on the `_v2` data, description only). An exponential distribution has exactly 1. So the survival curves are almost exponential, and with one birth
rule for all structures r is almost a function of V alone.

### What S4 shows
1. **In these passive linear worlds, the growth rate adds almost nothing to the mean exit time.** Lives are close to
   exponential, so r ranks structures like V (~~0.90 to 0.99~~ 0.95 to 0.99, phase C `_v2`). The ROADMAP gate ("if they disagree strongly") does not trigger.
2. **V is the slightly more stable ranking** when the viability limit changes (G2, ~~not rerun in phase C: old exit check~~
   night run, `_v2`: −0.066 [−0.076, −0.056], old −0.04 [−0.05, −0.03]; stability 0.86 for V and 0.80 for r).
3. **Φ_TC predicts r as well as it predicts V** (~~+0.41 both~~ +0.35 and +0.34, phase C `_v2`).
4. **The Price equation now has a measured transmission term.** With a random-swap offspring map, selection favours a
   higher Φ_TC in ~~78 %~~ 74 % of worlds, and mutation pulls it back in 98 % of worlds. The transmission term is about ~~11~~ 7 times
   smaller than the selection term at a mutation rate of 0.1 (phase C, `_v2`).
5. **The growth rate differs from V only if the birth rule differs between structures.** That needs a resource or a
   mechanism of division, which these worlds do not have. This is the step to S6 (real division).

### Limits
- One birth rule for all structures, chosen on purpose. A birth rule from resources is not done.
- The offspring map (random swap, rate 0.1) is ours. The transmission term depends on it.
- Only the S1 baseline worlds, where lives are near exponential. Worlds with bistable units (S2) have non-exponential lives and could give a different answer.
